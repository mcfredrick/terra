## Research Worth Reading
- **[Grid-Orch: An LLM-Powered Orchestrator for Distribution Grid Simulation and Analytics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12728)** — Framework that connects Large Language Models with power system simulation via the Model Context Protocol (MCP), letting engineers perform distribution grid analysis through natural language. Addresses the projected shortage of 1.5 million power distribution engineers by 2030.
- **[Revisiting Voltage and Synchronization Stability Analysis in Converter-Integrated Weak Grids: Insights from Non-Minimum-Phase Zeros](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13061)** — Analyzes voltage and synchronization stability in weak grids with high penetration of converter-interfaced generators, challenging the conventional separation of these two stability problems. Shows why the short-circuit ratio (SCR) serves as a unified metric.
- **[Safe and Energy-Aware Decentralized PDE-Constrained Optimization-Based Control of Multi-UAVs for Persistent Wildfire Suppression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12779)** — Decentralized optimization framework for coordinating multiple UAVs in wildfire suppression, accounting for localization and motion uncertainties while minimizing energy consumption. Couples UAV motion with water deployment using a wildfire-specific control Lyapunov function.
- **[Impedance-Based VSC Unit Commitment with STATCOM Support under High IBG Penetration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13355)** — MISOCP framework for unit commitment that co-optimizes frequency-nadir constraints and voltage support via STATCOMs in grids dominated by inverter-based generation. Embeds frequency-nadir constraints through synthetic inertia modeling.

## Today's Synthesis

The convergence of two trends in this digest points to a concrete opportunity. [Grid-Orch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12728) demonstrates that LLMs connected via the Model Context Protocol can make power system simulation accessible through natural language, motivated by a projected shortage of 1.5 million distribution engineers by 2030. Meanwhile, the work on [impedance-based VSC unit commitment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13355) and the [revisited stability analysis for converter-integrated weak grids](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13061) show that the problems grid operators face are only getting harder — co-optimizing frequency response, voltage support, and synchronization stability in inverter-dominated systems requires increasingly sophisticated models. For an engineer pivoting into climate tech, the actionable idea is to build on this trajectory: take the stability formulations and MISOCP frameworks from recent research and expose them as MCP-compatible tools that Grid-Orch-style orchestrators can call. This would let less specialized engineers and planners query complex grid scenarios — "what happens to voltage stability if I add 200 MW of solar at this node?" — without needing to run PSCAD or DIgSILENT by hand. The gap is not in the math; it's in the interface layer between domain experts and the models they need.
