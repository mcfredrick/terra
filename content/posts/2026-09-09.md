## Research Worth Reading

- **[Perovskite Solar Is Having Another Moment](https://heatmap.news/climate-tech/perovskite-solar-moment)** — Perovskites could raise solar-cell efficiency, especially in silicon tandems, while potentially reducing manufacturing cost. The unresolved engineering work is long-term stability, lead containment, and scalable production; ML and manufacturing analytics can support faster materials screening and defect detection.
- **[Road map to clean energy using laser beam ignition of boron-hydrogen fusion](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/laser-and-particle-beams/article/road-map-to-clean-energy-using-boronhydrogen-fusion/8BE057DC1BC9E0A588FB3ABAA993078C)** — The paper maps the plasma conditions, laser parameters, target design, and engineering milestones needed for proton–boron-11 fusion energy. It is a research roadmap, not a demonstrated power plant, and still faces severe confinement, laser-efficiency, and materials challenges.

## Open Source Projects

- **[Iceland drills 4.7 km down into volcano to tap clean energy](https://phys.org/news/2017-05-iceland-drills-km-volcano-energy.html)** — This deep-geothermal drilling case study highlights wellbore materials, drilling fluids, pressure control, and heat-exchanger design in exceptionally hot volcanic formations. It is a useful systems-engineering example, although commercial reliability and heat-extraction economics remain difficult.
- **[Oklo launches Aurora advanced fission clean energy plant in US](https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/news/oklo-launches-aurora-plant/)** — Oklo’s Aurora is a sodium-cooled, fast-spectrum small modular reactor designed for passive safety and on-site recycling of spent fuel into new fuel. Engineers would recognize transferable work in controls, thermal hydraulics, nuclear instrumentation, and regulatory-grade software.
- **[Sigenergy-Local-Modbus](https://github.com/TypQxQ/Sigenergy-Local-Modbus)** — This unofficial Home Assistant integration exposes Sigenergy battery data over local Modbus-TCP, including state of charge, charge or discharge power, and fault status. It is a concrete example of turning proprietary battery telemetry into a locally controlled, configurable monitoring stack.
- **[Questionable data center forecasts are driving up Ohio power bills](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/data-centers/questionable-data-center-forecasts-ohio-power-bills)** — The article examines how utilities use large data-center load forecasts in rate cases and long-term grid planning, even when some proposed facilities may not be built. Engineers can apply uncertainty quantification, staged procurement, auditable metering, and transparent demand forecasts to reduce financial and grid-planning risk.

## Today's Synthesis

Engineers pivoting into climate work can turn the battery telemetry exposed by [Sigenergy-Local-Modbus](https://github.com/TypQxQ/Sigenergy-Local-Modbus) into a test bed for the uncertainty-aware demand forecasting discussed in the [Questionable data center forecasts are driving up Ohio power bills](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/data-centers/questionable-data-center-forecasts-ohio-power-bills) article. By pulling state-of-charge and charge/discharge power data over local Modbus-TCP, a small-scale model can simulate how distributed storage responds to the questionable data-center load forecasts that utilities embed in rate cases. Pairing this with the [accessible satellite products and sensor networks](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=15132145) listed in the [Ask HN: What are some good sources of climate data to analyze?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=15132145) allows engineers to build labeled datasets for training regression models that quantify forecast error against real-time grid conditions. The resulting prototype would demonstrate how open-source APIs, local Modbus instrumentation, and uncertainty quantification techniques from grid-planning rate cases can be combined into a reproducible pipeline for demand-side management—moving from proprietary telemetry to auditable, locally controlled energy decisions without waiting for utility-scale rollouts.
