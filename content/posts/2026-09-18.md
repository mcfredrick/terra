## Research Worth Reading
- [PowerModels-ACOPF-AI: On-the-Fly Machine Learning Approach for Solving AC Optimal Power Flow Integrating Renewable Energy Sources](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19360) — Presents an ML approach for AC optimal power flow under high renewable penetration, targeting the scalability limits of traditional solvers. Relevant for engineers working on grid optimization where speed and uncertainty matter.
- [Resilient Design and Optimal Operation of Battery Energy Storage Systems for Behind-the-Meter Data Center Microgrids](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19342) — Proposes disaggregated facility load modeling at second- and minute-level resolution plus a multi-phase optimization framework for BESS planning and resilient operation. Useful if you're interested in data center energy resilience and storage sizing under high-fidelity load profiles.
- [Design of Economic Dispatch Schemes of An Isolated BESS Network Based on Distributed Discrete-time PI+Rest Consensus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19804) — Develops two distributed economic dispatch schemes for isolated battery networks using discrete-time PI+rest consensus, accounting for capacity degradation and power loss. A good reference for distributed control and multi-agent coordination in microgrids.
- [Carbon capture more costly than switching to renewables, researchers find](https://techxplore.com/news/2025-02-carbon-capture-renewables.html) — Reports a quantitative cost comparison between carbon capture and storage and renewable energy deployment. Provides useful context for where CCS does and doesn't make economic sense.
- [Efficient method to capture carbon dioxide from the atmosphere](https://www.helsinki.fi/en/news/innovations/efficient-method-capture-carbon-dioxide-atmosphere-developed-university-helsinki) — Details a new material or method for direct air capture from the University of Helsinki. Worth a look for the chemistry and claimed efficiency gains over incumbent DAC approaches.

## Today's Synthesis

The through-line across today's items is that ML's role in climate is only as useful as the economic and physical constraints it's embedded in. The [Georgia Tech/Meta open dataset](https://news.gatech.edu/news/2024/05/02/georgia-tech-and-meta-create-massive-open-dataset-advance-ai-solutions-carbon) gives you millions of molecular structures for training models to discover carbon capture materials — but as the [cost comparison between CCS and renewables](https://techxplore.com/news/2025-02-carbon-capture-renewables.html) makes clear, a high-performing sorbent that can't approach the cost curve of renewables plus storage won't deploy. A concrete project: build a screening pipeline that combines a graph neural network trained on the Georgia Tech dataset with a levelized-cost model, filtering candidate materials on both performance and economic viability from the start. The same pattern shows up in [ML for AC optimal power flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19360) — speed matters only if the solution respects grid operational constraints. For an engineer pivoting into climate, the differentiator isn't just training the model; it's knowing which problems have a credible path to deployment, and designing evaluation pipelines that test for that from day one.
