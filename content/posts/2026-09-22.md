## Research Worth Reading
- [Learning Control Policies from Heterogeneous Multi-Horizon Time Series in Battery Energy Management Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22546) — Introduces Representation-to-Decision (R2D), an imitation learning framework mapping heterogeneous multi-horizon time-series inputs directly to battery control decisions via modular temporal feature extractors and shared latent representation, bypassing explicit load modeling. Engineers pivoting into climate should care because it enables end-to-end learned control for BESS without manual feature engineering for load forecasting.
- [Communication Delay Robust Control of BESS for AI Training Load Smoothing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22542) — Develops a communication-delay robust control strategy for battery energy storage systems in data centers to mitigate power fluctuations from AI training loads, which differ between computational and communication phases. Engineers should care because it addresses practical control problems under network latency in grid-interactive data centers.
- [Intelligent Degradation Monitoring in Lithium-ion Batteries via Discharge Incremental Capacity Feature Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22843) — Estimates incremental capacity features from regular discharge data to detect lithium-ion battery degradation without requiring low-current cycling protocols. Engineers should care because it makes degradation monitoring deployable in real-world EVs and storage systems where cycling restrictions are impractical.
- [Adapting Boltz-2 with limited experimental activity data improves early enrichment in virtual screening](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24302) — Fine-tunes the Boltz-2 foundation model with sparse assay data to improve early enrichment of active compounds in virtual screening. Engineers should care because parameter-efficient adaptation of foundation models with limited experimental data is directly applicable to discovering novel materials and enzymes for carbon capture and biofuels.
- [Relationship between Loss of Load Expectation and Frequency of Load Loss Events](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22646) — Proves that Loss of Load Expectation (LOLE) can serve as a frequency metric under restricted conditions, differing from standard reliability frequency metrics. Engineers should care for power system reliability modeling and capacity planning where event frequency matters more than expected days of loss.
- [QLoRA Fine-Tuning of Ministral LLM for Sequence-to-Function Protein Annotation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24538) — Applies QLoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning to the Ministral LLM for sequence-to-function protein annotation, addressing bottlenecks in identifying enzymes for biofuel production and carbon capture. Engineers should care because it demonstrates how small, efficiently fine-tuned LLMs can accelerate protein engineering for sustainability applications.
