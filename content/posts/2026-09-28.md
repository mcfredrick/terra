## Today's Synthesis

Three recent arXiv preprints reveal a pipeline for engineer-driven grid modernization: a three-stage optimization framework for scheduling mobile energy storage systems to boost distribution-grid resilience during extreme weather ([Resilience Enhancement of Distribution Grids Through a Three-Stage Framework for Scheduling Mobile Energy Storage Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30606)); a decentralized voltage-control method using local trajectory convolution that dispenses with real-time communication for grids with high DER penetration ([Decentralized Gain Learning for Voltage Control via Local Trajectory Convolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30602)); and a decision-gated surrogate-assisted stochastic optimizer with certified bounds for assessing photovoltaic hosting capacity under irradiance and demand uncertainty ([Decision-Gated Surrogate-Assisted Stochastic Optimization with Independent High-Fidelity Certification for Photovoltaic Hosting-Capacity Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30847)). An engineer-actionable next step is to prototype a modular control stack that embeds the trajectory-convolution voltage regulator at the edge, feeds its measurements into the surrogate model for real-time hosting-capacity estimates, and triggers mobile ESS dispatch from the three-stage optimizer when resilience margins thin. This workflow lets software and ML engineers apply optimization, surrogate modeling, and decentralized control patterns to actual grid-infrastructure problems today, rather than waiting for utility-scale rollouts.
