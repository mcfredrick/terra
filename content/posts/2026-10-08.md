## Research Worth Reading

- **[Quantifying Grid-Forming Requirements for System Strength: How Much and Where?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08948)** — This paper quantifies the quantity and optimal placement of grid-forming inverters, which provide voltage-source characteristics to maintain system strength in IBR-dominated grids. It offers engineers quantitative requirements and location strategies relevant to grid modernization and planning.
- **[Safe Control of Semi-Explicit Differential-Algebraic Systems: Control Barrier Functions with SOS Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09634)** — This work extends control barrier function (CBF) methods to semi-explicit differential-algebraic equations (DAEs) using sum-of-squares (SOS) verification to handle algebraic constraints that standard CBFs ignore. It is directly applicable to control engineers building secure and stable power electronic-grid interfaces.
- **[Learning Stability of Replay-Based Co-Optimization for Transmission Expansion under Strategic Bidding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09366)** — This study uses deep reinforcement learning to co-optimize transmission expansion while market participants learn bidding strategies, revealing coupled non-stationary dynamics in grid planning. It provides frameworks for robust grid planning and electricity market design for those modeling strategic interactions.
- **[AI Data Centers Meet Electrical Grids: A Review of Power Challenges and Coordinated Solutions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09060)** — This review examines how AI data center power-electronic loads affect electrical grids, arguing that annual consumption metrics cannot capture critical effects involving location, temporal variability, and controllability. It surveys coordinated solutions relevant to software engineers modeling energy loads or working on grid-AI infrastructure.
