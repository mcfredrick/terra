## Research Worth Reading
- **[High-level, efficient ocean modeling at all scales with Oceananigans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14148)** — Oceananigans is an open-source Julia framework for scalable ocean simulation across resolutions, enabling engineers to explore multi-scale climate dynamics and trainable model components with quantifiable uncertainty.
- **[Global Parameter Identification and Uncertainty Quantification of reduced-order ADM1 using Maximum Likelihood Estimation and Bayesian Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19098)** — This work presents a globally convergent MLE and Bayesian pipeline for calibrating reduced-order ADM1 models, offering engineers a rigorous approach to parameter estimation and uncertainty quantification from sparse biogas production data.
- **[Physiology-Informed Sparse Identification of Dynamic Crop Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18920)** — This framework integrates physiological constraints into sparse identification of dynamic crop models, allowing engineers to capture plant growth dynamics under environmental forcing with high accuracy even under limited data.
- **[A Decoupled Approach for Modeling N-k Interdiction with Induced Failures in Electric Power Grids](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19289)** — This decoupled attacker-defender framework separates vulnerability identification from induced failure propagation, enabling scalable analysis of cascading outages in electric power grids under multiple attack scenarios, relevant for engineers assessing grid resilience and security.
- **[Njord: A Probabilistic Graph Neural Network for Ensemble Ocean Forecasting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15470)** — Njord introduces a probabilistic, data-driven framework combining deep latent variables with graph neural networks to produce ensemble ocean forecasts, directly addressing uncertainty and chaos for improved risk assessment and operational planning.
- **[A Performative Control Approach to Day-to-Day Traffic Signal Retiming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19022)** — This performative control framework models driver adaptation to signal policies as a feedback loop, improving traffic signal retiming efficiency and reducing stoppage times when applied to day-to-day traffic data, relevant for transportation-focused climate mitigation.

## Today's Synthesis

These recent advances provide complementary capabilities for engineers tackling complex energy-water-climate interactions. **Oceananigans** offers a mature, open-source Julia platform for large-scale ocean simulations across spatial and temporal resolutions, supporting the exploration of regional variability and extreme events critical for coastal and maritime operations. Meanwhile, **Njord** brings a probabilistic deep learning architecture—combining deep latent variable models with graph neural networks—to generate ensemble ocean forecasts with calibrated uncertainty estimates. Together, these tools enable a more principled workflow: use Oceananigans to simulate plausible physical boundary conditions and scenario ensembles, then feed those outputs into Njord-style probabilistic forecasting pipelines to produce statistically robust uncertainty bounds. For example, coastal managers can leverage Oceananigans-driven boundary condition sweeps to explore future sea-surface state distributions, and then apply Njord-inspired uncertainty quantification to translate those physical scenarios into probabilistic risk assessments for infrastructure planning and early warning systems. The key insight is that these methods address different but synergistic challenges—scalable simulation versus uncertainty-aware projection—and their integration yields decision-support frameworks better suited to real-world engineering applications than either method alone.
