## Research Worth Reading
- [Atmospheric Chemistry-Climate Modeling with an AI Foundation Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22555) — Proposes an AI foundation model to replace process-level schemes in Earth system models, targeting atmospheric composition and chemistry-climate interactions that are typically simplified due to computational cost. An engineer could build on this by developing evaluation frameworks or physics-informed training methods for learned climate representations.

- [Characterization, ageing and post-mortem dataset of commercial 58Ah prismatic NMC cells](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23049) — Presents a comprehensive open dataset of GITT, HPPC, EIS characterization, aging results from 38 distinct conditions, and post-mortem teardown analysis for 58 Ah prismatic NMC cells. Battery engineers and ML researchers can use this dataset to train predictive models for cycle life and degradation mechanisms at scale.


- [Filter-Augmented Reinforcement Learning for Battery Fast Charging with State of Health and Plating-Aware Reward Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22787) — Proposes a filter-augmented RL framework built on TD3 for lifelong fast charging that incorporates state-of-health awareness and plating-aware reward shaping to balance speed against degradation. Directly applicable to engineers working on RL-based battery management systems for electric vehicles.

- [Energy-Aware Earth System Science: A Community Roadmap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23216) — Analyzes energy demands across 159 CMIP models, finding model error decreases ~13.5% per doubling of energy use. Offers a roadmap for energy-aware model development and quality assessment, relevant to engineers designing climate infrastructure under growing compute constraints.

- [Exploiting the Flexibility of LLM Inference for Frequency Regulation Service](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23532) — Investigates using LLM inference clusters as controllable loads for frequency regulation, exploiting the unique execution characteristics of LLM workloads to provide grid-responsive power flexibility. Demonstrates how general-compute infrastructure can participate in demand response, useful for energy systems engineers exploring distributed grid services.

## Technology & Innovation

Commercial Scale Is A Much Bigger System.](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/10/19/verdox-carbon-capture-aluminum-economics/) — Verdox demonstrated electrochemical CO₂ capture at Hydro's Sunndal aluminum smelter over two months, capturing carbon from exhaust with ~1% concentration while operating amid typical smelter contaminants. Validates real-world capture feasibility at industrial scale, relevant to engineers evaluating carbon capture deployment pathways for heavy industry.

## Open Source Projects
- [adithyavemuri/WRF_tools](https://github.com/adithyavemuri/WRF_tools) — Scientific Python toolkit for WRF and WRF-LES data analysis and turbulence diagnostics, including OpenFAST coupling for wind energy resource assessment. Useful for atmospheric scientists and engineers working on boundary layer research and wind energy modeling pipelines.

## Today's Synthesis

An engineer pivoting into climate work could start by adapting the [AI foundation model for atmospheric chemistry-climate modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22555) to operate under the energy constraints quantified in the [Energy-Aware Earth System Science roadmap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23216). That roadmap reports a ~13.5% model error reduction per doubling of energy use, establishing a clear compute-efficiency trade-off. By incorporating physics-informed regularization and monitoring training energy via the roadmap’s assessment framework, the foundation model can be trained more sustainably. Separately, the [LLM inference cluster study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23532) demonstrates that general-purpose compute can serve as a controllable load for frequency regulation, exploiting workload characteristics to provide grid-responsive power flexibility. An actionable project would combine these: train the climate model under energy budgets that, when exceeded, automatically shift surplus inference capacity into frequency regulation service via the LLM framework, creating a closed loop between model development and grid interaction. This approach directly transfers ML systems skills—training loop instrumentation, reward shaping, and deployment on heterogeneous clusters—to the climate domain while respecting compute constraints.
