## Today's Synthesis

The DOE's BattMan accelerator, led by PNNL, is channeling federal R&D into next-generation battery chemistry for both EVs and grid-scale storage (see [Sure Looks Like Trump Loves EVs After All](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/10/29/battman-batteries-evs-energy-storage-us-trump/)). At the same time, PJM, the regional grid operator in the Mid-Atlantic, is explicitly calling for fast-tracked grid battery deployments to handle growing renewable integration (see [PJM desperately needs grid batteries. Here's a way it could get some.](https://www.canarymedia.com/news/pjm-desperately-needs-grid-batteries-heres-a-way-it-could-get-some/)). The BattMan program's focus on new materials and chemistry pathways aligns directly with PJM's need for scalable, cost-effective grid storage, connecting federal R&D investment to real-world deployment opportunities at PJM's most constrained nodes. This linkage between DOE-funded battery innovation and PJM's grid integration demands positions public funding toward advancing both EV charging infrastructure and grid stability through the next generation of battery technologies.
