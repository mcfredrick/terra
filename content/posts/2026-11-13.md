## Today's Synthesis

A practical pivot for software and systems engineers is to build the control layer that makes clean power usable under uncertainty. The offshore wind report [Managing Offshore Wind in the Face of Uncertainty](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/12/managing-offshore-wind-in-the-face-of-uncertainty/) shows that variable generation and siting constraints require adaptive operations, while the data center piece [Data Centers, AI, & the Growing Demand for Energy: Impacts, Risks, & Solutions](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/12/data-centers-ai-the-growing-demand-for-energy-impacts-risks-solutions/) shows that AI workloads are creating large, flexible, but poorly coordinated electricity demand. An engineer-actionable project is a forecasting-and-dispatch service that predicts offshore wind output, data center compute load, and grid carbon intensity, then schedules non-urgent training jobs, cooling, and storage around those forecasts. The system would use probabilistic time-series models, queue-aware workload placement, and safety constraints for latency-critical inference. It could also expose APIs for operators to simulate curtailment, reserve margins, and demand-response events. This is not a research-only exercise: it maps directly to existing skills in distributed systems, ML forecasting, optimization, and reliability engineering, and it addresses a real bottleneck in decarbonizing both AI infrastructure and variable renewables.
