## Research Worth Reading

- **[Fabric Molds Could Bring Material-Saving Concrete Beams Within Reach](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/14/fabric-molds-could-bring-material-saving-concrete-beams-within-reach/)** — MIT engineers cast topology-optimized concrete beams in sewn fabric molds, cutting concrete use by roughly a third and reinforcing steel by half against conventional beams. The optimization side is familiar territory for anyone who's worked in structural or generative design; the interesting constraint is manufacturability, since the mold has to be sewn rather than milled.

- **[Study Pinpoints States with Steepest Warming Trends Since 1980](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/14/study-pinpoints-states-with-steepest-warming-trends-since-1980/)** — Ranks U.S. states by local warming trend since 1980, producing geographically specific signals for adaptation and infrastructure planning. A decent reference for the data work underneath most climate risk tooling: station ingestion, homogenization, and trend estimation with uneven spatial coverage.

## Technology & Innovation

- **[Sonnen, SOLRITE, & ADAPT Energy, Integrating VPP Breakers to Turn Home Loads into Flexible Grid Assets](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/14/sonnen-solrite-adapt-energy-integrating-vpp-breakers-to-turn-home-loads-into-flexible-grid-assets/)** — The three are building a breaker-level controller into a next-generation virtual power plant node, making individual residential circuits dispatchable rather than just metered in aggregate. The hard parts are distributed control and telemetry at the edge: fleets of heterogeneous devices, unreliable connectivity, and response times fast enough to count as a grid service.

## Open Source Projects

- **[Giorgio866/lumina-energy-card](https://github.com/Giorgio866/lumina-energy-card)** — A Home Assistant Lovelace card that renders electricity consumption, battery state, EV charging, and multiple PV arrays in one view. Small and self-contained, which makes it a low-friction entry point for learning the Home Assistant entity and data model.

- **[313 kW Rooftop Solar System at Virginia Elementary Will Save $581,000 in Energy Costs](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/14/313-kw-solar-power-system-rooftop-system-at-virginia-elementary-will-save-581000-in-energy-costs/)** — Secure Solar Futures is installing a 313 kW rooftop array at Preston Park Elementary in Roanoke, VA, projected to save $581,000 in energy costs. Not a code project, but a concrete anchor for the scale and payback math behind behind-the-meter deployments you'd be modeling or instrumenting.

## Today's Synthesis

The [state warming trends](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/14/study-pinpoints-states-with-steepest-warming-trends-since-1980/), the [breaker-level VPP controller](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/14/sonnen-solrite-adapt-energy-integrating-vpp-breakers-to-turn-home-loads-into-flexible-grid-assets/), and the [Home Assistant energy card](https://github.com/Giorgio866/lumina-energy-card) line up into one tractable project. The warming study is the input most flexibility models skip: homogenized, per-state trend estimates you can convert into cooling-degree-day shifts, which is exactly the signal that decides whether a residential flexibility fleet still clears its peak once the climate it was sized for moves. The VPP work tells you the asset is now dispatchable at circuit granularity, and the Home Assistant card shows what that telemetry surface looks like when it's open and local-first instead of locked behind a vendor API. Concretely: stand up a Home Assistant instance, treat its entities as a stand-in for breaker-level telemetry, and run a small dispatch or model-predictive-control loop against load shapes perturbed by the warming trends. You get a testable artifact, a feel for where the forecast degrades, and something that maps directly onto both the grid-services and climate-risk hiring paths — no retraining required.
