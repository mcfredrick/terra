## Research Worth Reading

**[Reliability-based Sizing of Energy Storage for Large Load Integration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25176)** — A planning framework for sizing energy storage to maintain grid reliability as large loads like AI data centers connect to bulk power systems. Uses probabilistic methods to quantify outage risk under rapid demand growth, relevant for engineers working on grid integration and capacity expansion models.

**[Comfort energy management system for electrified buildings using hydrogen for seasonal storage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04293)** — Proposes a building energy management system pairing short-term batteries with hydrogen-based seasonal storage, coupling fuel cells with heat pumps. Demonstrates how multi-timescale storage can align volatile renewable generation with heating/cooling demand while maintaining occupant comfort constraints.

**[Xaurora: Generative Weather Forecasting with Denoising Stochastic Interpolants from a Foundation Model Prior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06509)** — Generative weather forecasting framework using denoising stochastic interpolants conditioned on an atmospheric foundation model. Produces ensemble forecasts more efficiently than deterministic or pure physics-based approaches, relevant for ML engineers exploring probabilistic modeling in Earth system science.

**[Stochastic Crew Placement for Winter Storm Restoration of Rural Distribution Feeders Considering Building Cooling Deadlines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25223)** — Two-stage stochastic MILP for pre-positioning repair crews ahead of winter storms, with explicit constraints on building cooling deadlines for electrically heated homes. Combines grid resilience optimization with thermal dynamics of buildings — useful for operations research and power systems engineers.

Growth driven by co-location with solar and standalone grid services; relevant for engineers working on BESS integration, market participation models, and curtailment mitigation.

## Today's Synthesis

A concrete next step for engineers is to combine [reliability-based storage sizing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25176), [stochastic crew placement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25223), and [Xaurora’s generative ensemble forecasts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06509) into an open-source resilience-planning pipeline. Start by ingesting Xaurora ensemble weather outputs to generate scenario trees for storm-driven outages and building cooling/heating deadlines. Feed those scenarios into a two-stage stochastic MILP that co-optimizes BESS sizing/dispatch and repair-crew pre-positioning, with reliability constraints from the storage-sizing framework. Then validate against real installation and outage data, using [U.S. battery storage growth](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/batteries/another-banner-year-us-energy-storage-installations) as a demand-side check on whether proposed BESS capacity is plausible. The actionable contribution is a modular codebase: forecast adapter, scenario generator, optimization model, and evaluation harness. This lets power-systems and OR engineers test how storage placement changes crew routing costs and outage risk, rather than treating them as separate planning problems. It also creates a benchmark for comparing deterministic versus probabilistic forecasts in climate adaptation.
