## Technology & Innovation
- [Ford Begins Producing Its Own LFP Batteries](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/21/ford-begins-producing-its-own-lfp-batteries/) — Ford has started manufacturing lithium iron phosphate cells at BlueOval Battery Park Michigan under a technology license from CATL, bringing cobalt-free, lower-cost chemistry in-house. Relevant if you work on battery management systems, cell-to-pack integration, or manufacturing data pipelines — LFP's flat voltage curve and different degradation profile change what the BMS actually has to estimate.

## Open Source Projects
- [The Deadly Shipwreck Threatening Offshore Wind](https://heatmap.news/plus/the-fight/hotspots/the-deadly-shipwreck-threatening-offshore-wind) — A legacy wreck near a proposed offshore wind site is complicating seabed survey, cable routing, and foundation siting, with possible munitions and fuel oil involved. A concrete example of the geospatial constraint-mapping and marine survey data problems that sit underneath every offshore project — the kind of work that rewards GIS, remote sensing, and data engineering skills.

## Community Finds
- [Europe’s Modelled €5.70/kg Hydrogen Faces Industrial Buyers Who Can Afford Far Less](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/21/europe-hydrogen-cost-industrial-buyers-price-ceilings/) — Modelled European production costs for hydrogen sit well above what industrial buyers in ammonia, steel, and high-temperature heat can actually pay, and those buyers have competing decarbonization routes. Useful grounding for anyone doing techno-economic modeling or optimization in the space: the binding constraint is often the offtake price ceiling, not the production cost curve.

## Today's Synthesis

Two of today's items are the same problem seen from opposite ends. [Europe's modelled €5.70/kg hydrogen](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/21/europe-hydrogen-cost-industrial-buyers-price-ceilings/) shows the binding constraint is the offtake price ceiling, not the production cost curve — and offshore wind is one of the few feedstocks that could push electricity cheap enough to move that curve. But whether a given offshore site pencils out depends on the seabed realities in [the shipwreck complicating an offshore wind site](https://heatmap.news/plus/the-fight/hotspots/the-deadly-shipwreck-threatening-offshore-wind): survey cost, cable routing, foundation siting. The engineer-actionable idea is to couple the two. Build a model that takes marine survey and constraint-mapping data (GIS, remote sensing) and outputs a per-site levelized cost of hydrogen, so developers can screen which sites clear the buyer price ceiling before committing to expensive seabed surveys. If you bring geospatial data engineering plus optimization or techno-economic modeling skills, this is a concrete niche: the spatial-constraint layer and the cost model are usually built by separate teams, and joining them is the actual work.
