## Research Worth Reading
- [Loop-OPF: Solver-Informed Learning for Optimal Power Flow with Inference-Time Topology Adaptivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27013) — Presents a solver-informed learning method for optimal power flow that combines neural predictions with optimization structure to improve feasibility and optimality. Its inference-time topology adaptivity matters for grid operations where network configuration can change.
- [$\mathcal{H}_\infty$ Grid-Forming Control in DC Microgrids](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26827) — Studies H-infinity grid-forming control for DC microgrids to improve DC-bus voltage regulation and transient stability as DC generation, storage, and loads grow. It addresses limitations of conventional droop control and is relevant to power electronics and microgrid control design.
- [Which Data-Center Load Models Can Be Trusted? An Evidence-Graded Review of Dynamic Modeling and Validation for Large Computational Loads](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27169) — Reviews dynamic load models for data centers and large computational loads, grading the evidence behind their use in grid ride-through and disturbance-response studies. It matters for engineers working on grid integration of large flexible loads and data-center demand.
- [Unlocking Heterogeneous Accelerator Flexibility in AI Data Centers: Sizing and Workload Allocation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27576) — Studies strategic sizing and workload allocation for heterogeneous AI accelerators under local grid capacity constraints. It connects energy-efficiency tradeoffs, capital costs, and workload flexibility to data-center demand management and grid-aware deployment.
