## Research Worth Reading
- [Europe’s Inland Shipping Models Underprice Green Fuels. Market Prices Favor Battery Swapping.](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/25/inland-shipping-hvo-prices-battery-swapping/) — PLATINA4Action modeling compares HVO propulsion and battery swapping for a 110-meter dry-cargo vessel. Using observed October 2026 Dutch HVO prices, battery swapping is €551/day cheaper, which matters for energy-cost modeling and inland shipping decarbonization.
- [Toxic Port Pollution Costs Europe €2.5bn A Year In Health & Environmental Damages — Study](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/24/toxic-port-pollution-costs-europe-e2-5bn-a-year-in-health-environmental-damages-study/) — The study estimates that commercial ships docked in European ports cause €2.5 billion annually in societal and environmental damages. It highlights sulfur emissions from berthed vessels as a major hidden cost, making it relevant for environmental impact assessment, port regulation, and emissions-reduction engineering.

## Community Finds

## Today's Synthesis

A practical project is an open-source inland-port decarbonization planner that connects vessel energy choices to grid and health-cost constraints. Start with the cost model in [Europe’s Inland Shipping Models Underprice Green Fuels. Market Prices Favor Battery Swapping.](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/25/inland-shipping-hvo-prices-battery-swapping/) and extend it with berth-level shore-power availability, battery-swap queue times, and local grid capacity. Add the damage-cost framing from [The True Cost of In-Port Emissions](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/24/the-true-cost-of-in-port-emissions/) so operators can compare €/day fuel savings against avoided sulfur emissions and health burdens. Use [Rotterdam’s Methanol Expansion Is Not an Inland Shipping Fuel Strategy](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/24/rotterdams-methanol-expansion-is-not-an-inland-shipping-fuel-strategy/) as a case study: show why methanol infrastructure may not serve regular inland routes, while battery swapping and shore power can. Engineers could contribute scheduling algorithms, battery state-of-charge forecasting, port load profiles, and emissions accounting modules. The output would be a decision dashboard for port authorities: which vessels should electrify, where swap stations or shore-power upgrades give the highest avoided-cost benefit for inland freight corridors.
