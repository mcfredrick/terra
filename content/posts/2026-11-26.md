## Research Worth Reading 🍂

- **[Optimization of a Hybrid Solar-Biogas-Battery Energy Storage Microgrid: A Case Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33593)** 🦃 — Techno-economic optimization of the Soroti microgrid in eastern Uganda, combining concentrating solar power, biogas, and battery storage where grid access is thin. It's a capacity-sizing and dispatch problem — the same class of constrained optimization work as any resource planner you've built, just with weather and fuel in the loop.
- **[Basin-Scale Nonlinear Dynamics of Thermohaline Ocean Circulation. Part I: Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33383)** 🌽 — A unifying theoretical framework derived from tracer conservation that accounts for the multiple equilibria and abrupt transitions showing up across different ocean models. If you like dynamical systems and bifurcation analysis, this is climate modeling at the level of the equations rather than the output files.
- **[Voltage-Feedback Backoff and Restoration for Priority-Based EV Charging Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33300)** 🥧 — A centralized controller that prevents sustained undervoltage in residential distribution networks by separating *how many* EVs charge concurrently from *which* ones get selected, then adjusting the allowable count from monitored critical voltages. Feedback control plus priority scheduling — familiar territory for anyone who has written a rate limiter or a load shedder.
- **[COSIMA Cookbook: a community-driven resource for ocean and sea-ice modelling science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30182)** 🍂 — Open Jupyter notebook workflows for analyzing ocean and sea-ice model output, built for large gridded datasets like ACCESS. Free, reproducible, and the fastest way to learn the data-handling half of climate science (xarray, dask, chunked netCDF) before committing to a pivot.

## Open Source Projects 🦃

- **[mtrossbach/noah-mqtt](https://github.com/mtrossbach/noah-mqtt)** 🌽 — A Go tool that bridges Growatt Noah 2000 home batteries to Home Assistant over MQTT, exposing monitoring and control inside a local smart-home stack. Small and readable enough to use as a template for local-first energy tooling with no cloud dependency and no vendor API in the path.

## Implementation Guides 🥧

- **[Plug-in Solar UL Certification for US Safety Regs — What UL 3700 Does & Doesn't Say](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/26/plug-in-solar-ul-certification-for-us-safety-regs-what-ul-3700-does-doesnt-say/)** 🍂 — Breaks down the scope and the gaps in UL 3700, the standard governing plug-in and balcony solar that feeds panels through a microinverter into a standard wall outlet. Useful if you're evaluating grid interconnection, anti-islanding behavior, and what certification actually demands for renter-friendly distributed generation.

## Today's Synthesis

Before the Thanksgiving leftovers crowd the fridge, build a local-first load governor for your home energy stack. Use [mtrossbach/noah-mqtt](https://github.com/mtrossbach/noah-mqtt) to expose battery state over MQTT, then implement the voltage-feedback backoff idea from [Voltage-Feedback Backoff and Restoration for Priority-Based EV Charging Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33300) as a small scheduler: treat critical loads like dishes—oven, EV charger, heat pump—as priority classes, cap concurrent draw from monitored voltage or inverter limits, and restore capacity gradually. Add a safe plug-in solar path only after checking [Plug-in Solar UL Certification for US Safety Regs](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/26/plug-in-solar-ul-certification-for-us-safety-regs-what-ul-3700-does-doesnt-say/), because anti-islanding and outlet-level limits are not optional side dishes. The engineer-friendly prototype is a Python or Go service subscribing to MQTT telemetry, computing an allowed concurrent count from voltage thresholds, and publishing setpoints. Start with simulation using synthetic voltage traces, then test on a bench supply before touching real wiring. Log every decision so you can compare policy changes against voltage excursions. This gives you a tangible climate-adjacent control problem: local, measurable, and deployable without cloud dependencies.
