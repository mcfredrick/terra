Today’s Synthesis

The common thread across Qnetic’s underground flywheel storage and the Sherco coal plant conversion is grid interconnection reuse — the hardest, slowest part of deploying new storage. Sherco demonstrates the playbook: keep the substation, transformers, and transmission rights; swap the generation. Flywheels fit this same model but with distinct mechanical advantages: 20+ year cycle life without degradation, no thermal runaway risk, and domestic supply chains for steel and composites. For mechanical and systems engineers, the actionable path is designing flywheel modules that standardize on retired plant voltage levels (13.8–34.5 kV) and footprint constraints (turbine hall floors, cooling water access). The Loganair/BETA electric aircraft order extends this pattern to airports: many sit on former military bases with robust grid connections. A flywheel buffer at an airport substation could absorb 500 kW–2 MW aircraft charging pulses without demand charges or distribution upgrades — same mechanical package, different site. The engineering challenge across all three: standardize the grid-side power conversion and controls so the rotating mass becomes a plug-compatible storage block for any brownfield interconnection.
