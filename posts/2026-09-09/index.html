Research Worth Reading

  • Perovskite Solar Is Having Another Moment — Perovskites could raise solar-cell efficiency, especially in silicon tandems, while potentially reducing manufacturing cost. The unresolved engineering work is long-term stability, lead containment, and scalable production; ML and manufacturing analytics can support faster materials screening and defect detection.
  • Road map to clean energy using laser beam ignition of boron-hydrogen fusion — The paper maps the plasma conditions, laser parameters, target design, and engineering milestones needed for proton–boron-11 fusion energy. It is a research roadmap, not a demonstrated power plant, and still faces severe confinement, laser-efficiency, and materials challenges.

Technology & Innovation

  • What Lithium EV Battery Shortage? New Recycler Picks Up Where Ascend Elements Left Off — A Georgia recycling operation is restoring domestic recovery of lithium carbonate from spent EV batteries. Engineers would care about process control, contamination handling, yield tracking, and the economics of closing the battery-materials loop.
  • Vertical Aerospace’s eVTOL Can Fly. The Passenger Business Is Still Unproven — Vertical Aerospace demonstrated transition between vertical and wingborne flight, advancing the control, propulsion, and certification work required for eVTOL aircraft. The remaining challenge is not only airworthiness but also fleet utilization, maintenance cost, noise, and passenger economics.
  • Ask HN: Local implications and economic impact of datacenters? — This discussion covers data-center cooling, power efficiency, grid load, and renewable integration rather than presenting a single validated result. It is relevant to engineers working on telemetry, capacity planning, demand response, and quantifying local energy impacts.

Open Source Projects

  • Iceland drills 4.7 km down into volcano to tap clean energy — This deep-geothermal drilling case study highlights wellbore materials, drilling fluids, pressure control, and heat-exchanger design in exceptionally hot volcanic formations. It is a useful systems-engineering example, although commercial reliability and heat-extraction economics remain difficult.
  • Oklo launches Aurora advanced fission clean energy plant in US — Oklo’s Aurora is a sodium-cooled, fast-spectrum small modular reactor designed for passive safety and on-site recycling of spent fuel into new fuel. Engineers would recognize transferable work in controls, thermal hydraulics, nuclear instrumentation, and regulatory-grade software.
  • Sigenergy-Local-Modbus — This unofficial Home Assistant integration exposes Sigenergy battery data over local Modbus-TCP, including state of charge, charge or discharge power, and fault status. It is a concrete example of turning proprietary battery telemetry into a locally controlled, configurable monitoring stack.
  • Questionable data center forecasts are driving up Ohio power bills — The article examines how utilities use large data-center load forecasts in rate cases and long-term grid planning, even when some proposed facilities may not be built. Engineers can apply uncertainty quantification, staged procurement, auditable metering, and transparent demand forecasts to reduce financial and grid-planning risk.

Today’s Synthesis

Engineers pivoting into climate work can turn the battery telemetry exposed by Sigenergy-Local-Modbus into a test bed for the uncertainty-aware demand forecasting discussed in the Questionable data center forecasts are driving up Ohio power bills article. By pulling state-of-charge and charge/discharge power data over local Modbus-TCP, a small-scale model can simulate how distributed storage responds to the questionable data-center load forecasts that utilities embed in rate cases. Pairing this with the accessible satellite products and sensor networks listed in the Ask HN: What are some good sources of climate data to analyze? allows engineers to build labeled datasets for training regression models that quantify forecast error against real-time grid conditions. The resulting prototype would demonstrate how open-source APIs, local Modbus instrumentation, and uncertainty quantification techniques from grid-planning rate cases can be combined into a reproducible pipeline for demand-side management—moving from proprietary telemetry to auditable, locally controlled energy decisions without waiting for utility-scale rollouts.
