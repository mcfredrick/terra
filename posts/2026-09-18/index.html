Today’s Synthesis

The through-line across today’s items is that ML’s role in climate is only as useful as the economic and physical constraints it’s embedded in. The Georgia Tech/Meta open dataset gives you millions of molecular structures for training models to discover carbon capture materials — but as the cost comparison between CCS and renewables makes clear, a high-performing sorbent that can’t approach the cost curve of renewables plus storage won’t deploy. A concrete project: build a screening pipeline that combines a graph neural network trained on the Georgia Tech dataset with a levelized-cost model, filtering candidate materials on both performance and economic viability from the start. The same pattern shows up in ML for AC optimal power flow — speed matters only if the solution respects grid operational constraints. For an engineer pivoting into climate, the differentiator isn’t just training the model; it’s knowing which problems have a credible path to deployment, and designing evaluation pipelines that test for that from day one.
